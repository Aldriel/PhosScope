PhosScope compares phosphorylation between a control group and a disease group.

1. Data
Open a MaxQuant file such as Phospho (STY)Sites.txt, a FragPipe table whose columns end with Intensity, or any delimited table with an identifier and sample columns. The simulated example is invented by PhosScope: it is not patient data.

Assign each sample to Control or Disease. The program guesses the group from the sample name (control, temoin, disease, patient, tumor…).

2. Cleaning
Contaminants, reversed decoy hits and peptides identified only as a modified site are dropped. A localization probability under 0.75 is dropped too. An AScore, which is larger than 1, is not filtered by that threshold. A site must be measured at least twice in each group.

3. Imputation
Zero and empty cells are missing values. Perseus replaces them, on the log2 scale, by a draw from a normal law 1.8 standard deviations below the sample mean and 0.3 times as wide. The same seed always gives the same values. Half-minimum fills with one log2 unit under the lowest observed value. None keeps only complete sites.

4. Results
A Welch t-test compares the groups without assuming equal variances. p-values are adjusted by Benjamini-Hochberg. A site is significant when its q-value and its absolute log2 fold change both pass the thresholds. The volcano plot shows log2 fold change and -log10(p).

5. Report
The PDF states the filters, the imputation and the significant sites. It is a starting point for a biologist, not a clinical diagnosis.
